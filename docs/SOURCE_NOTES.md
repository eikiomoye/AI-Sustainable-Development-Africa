# Source notes and interpretation limits

## Included studies and bibliography

Section 3.3 reports 26 included studies after PRISMA-guided selection. The final bibliography has 72 entries. Original search exports, duplicate decisions, full-text screening records, the 26-study extraction sheet and coding records were not supplied. Neither bibliography membership nor a mention in a synthesis table proves inclusion in the final 26-study set.

The stage counts and exclusion-reason totals reconcile arithmetically. That check verifies the reported counts' consistency, not the existence, completeness or correctness of every original screening decision. The paper references a supplementary PRISMA checklist, but the supplement was not included in the supplied PDF or retrieved for this companion.

This is a sample of mismatches, not an exhaustive correction list. It supports caution about reconstructing article-level study membership or detailed evidence mappings from Table 2. The companion's sector catalogue therefore indexes domains and themes rather than assigning individual studies to an asserted included set.

## Search period and cited sources

Section 3.2 states a January-April 2026 search period with a primary publication focus of 2013-2024 and some foundational exceptions. The final bibliography also includes 2025 and 2026 sources, for example [9], [10], [16] and [26]. The companion preserves that information and does not invent a year-based inclusion filter or assume which sources were background rather than included studies.

## Framework status

Section 8 and the conclusions describe the framework as synthesis-based and conceptual, not empirically validated. The discussion worksheet, descriptive state vocabulary and Python utility were developed later for this repository. No benchmark, country score, field deployment or effectiveness finding is claimed.

The paper distinguishes technical performance, pilots, sustained deployment and development outcomes. The companion preserves that distinction. Hypothesised benefits and illustrative sectors should not be described as demonstrated improvements across African contexts.
